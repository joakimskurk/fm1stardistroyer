▶ Play it in the browser · Hear the demos · Getting started

Star Distroyer turns the FM-1 into one instrument with four engines in a row. A voice of sixteen oscillators, morphed across an X/Y field; a Reactor of four resonators that sweep through vowels, chords and combs; DISTROY, which splits the sound into four bands and wrecks each its own way; and TIME and SPACE, a delay and a looper feeding a plate. Around them: a modulation matrix with RADAR (a loop of random steps), four macros, a two-track step sequencer with segments and ORBIT over it, and an arpeggiator.

Hear it

SINGULARITY and RANGE are each one take on one FM-1, played the way one person plays it: the app's own controls on their pages, the voice rules, nothing mixed in. SINGULARITY is also the FM-1's own demo, on SETUP. Both are rendered on a computer by the firmware's own sound engine, the same C the FM-1 runs. The picture under each is its spectrum, low to high, from start to end: tap it to jump.

Try it here

The firmware itself, compiled for the browser: the same C the FM-1 runs, its sound, its screen and its lights, on a panel laid out as the FM-1's. Turn it on and play: the keys with the mouse or a finger, the computer's keyboard or a MIDI keyboard; drag or scroll a knob to turn it. A click on GLO keeps it until the next (GLO lit). What SAVE keeps stays in this browser.

POWER ON starts it: the firmware loads (a third of a megabyte) and plays here.

The CONSOLE shows everything at once on one big screen, as it plays: the chain from the voice to SPACE, the nav, the macros and RADAR, both tracks, ORBIT and ARP, the matrix. It follows an FM-1 over USB (Chrome or Edge), or a player of its own.

Getting started

The first minutes, on an FM-1 or in the player above. The player's computer keys are in brackets.

  1. Play. It starts on DISTROY, on the NAV page. Play the keys (A S D F … the white keys from C4, W E T Y U … the black ones, Z and X the octave), or a MIDI keyboard.
  2. Press PLAY (Space). SEQ A plays its starting line, a bass line in A minor: play over it. PLAY again stops it.
  3. Turn the macros. Press HOME until MACROS is the red tab on the top line. K1–K4 turn MUTATE, MOVE, BREAK and a fourth (BODY, AIR, DROWN or CUTOFF, as the patch chooses). At 0 the patch is as it was programmed.
  4. Find a patch. Turn PRESETS: a list opens, and the sound stays as it is. EDIT or OCT+ loads the one picked, OCT− or HOME keeps yours.
  5. Find your way. A page button goes to its page, and pressed again to its next one. K1–K4 turn the four values in the row on top, and ALGORITHM picks which four: the page's layers, a row of buttons on the bottom line, the one in use lit. EDIT turns what the page shows on or off. GLO (Shift) is what the keys do instead of playing: on SEQ the transpose, on ORBIT the steps.
  6. Write a pattern. On SEQ, SELECT picks a step: turn K1 for its note, K4 for its type (a rest, a tie, a slide). The keys play, so try a line first; then press REC and play them: stopped, step by step; playing, into the step that plays. OCT moves the keys' octave here too; GLO + OCT the track's transpose an octave.
  7. Keep it. Press SAVE, turn ALGORITHM to a place, and press SAVE again (or OCT+; OCT− lets it be). On SEQ it keeps the pattern. In the player, what SAVE keeps stays in this browser.

The sound

VOICE · four banks of four oscillators

  • Each oscillator is a harmonic 1–32 of the note with a fine tune, a carrier or a modulator.
  • XM sets the FM, WARP turns sine → triangle → saw → pulse, FOLD folds, DETUNE spreads.
  • The NAV X/Y blends the four banks; COURSE plays four stored X/Y cells from each note.
  • Harmonic generators re-roll a bank, from a list as the knob turns. MONO, DUO or POLY.

REACTOR · a quad morphing resonator

  • Four filters of one model: BP6, BP12, a variable BPF, KP combs, or two kinds of allpass.
  • Six stages hold every filter's pitch and level; POS moves through them, SLEW jumps or glides.
  • A stage clock and key tracking; HARM snaps the filters to the note's harmonics.
  • STAGE, a layer of its own: SELECT picks a stage, K1 puts a vowel or a chord in it, K2 to K4 its pitch, width and level; the keys play on.

DISTROY · multiband destruction

  • Four bands, LOW to TOP, each its TYPE (SOFT HARD FOLD CRUSH), DRIVE, what the type does (KNEE, SPACING, DEPTH), LEVEL; BIAS, PAN, DYNAMIC and BODY on SHAPE, SPLIT on MIX.
  • PHAT lifts quiet parts and tails, SHARP spits transients.
  • A feedback loop with TAME to darken it, and a limiter so it never blows up.

TIME · SPACE · a delay and a looper into a plate

  • TIME, the delay: stereo or ping-pong (an echo left, then one as loud right), free (1 ms – 1 s) or on the beat, with feedback and tone. On the beat it stays there: a division longer than 1 s plays at half (TIME red).
  • LOOPER, its own page (ARP's second): LOOP on its first layer, armed with EDIT or L-LEVEL (it borrows half the delay line: 5 s, a light 8-bit tape, the delay then 0.5 s), always recording what you hear, plays its last LENGTH round while the delay goes on; SPEED takes it an octave up or down, as a tape's (GLO + OCT the next octave, over TOOLS' GLIDE, landing on the loop's own grid), REV (TOOLS) plays it backwards; it goes into the reverb as the synth does. It follows PLAY: STOP stops the loop too, and PLAY starts it again in sync with the sequencer.
  • SPACE, the plate: four diffusers into a tank in a figure of eight, its lines gently moving. SIZE 0.35–1.75 x (it glides there like tape), DECAY 0.3 s to about 40 s, TONE.
  • FREEZE (SPACE's FREEZE layer, K1): the plate's tail held as it is, the input closed; play over it. PITCH (K2 there) takes the frozen tail up or down; in MOD it is FREEZE PITCH, and SEQ A or B there at 100 % plays it as a melody. SHIMMER (SPACE's MORE layer): an octave up of the tail back into it.

MOVEMENT · macros and the matrix

  • Six macros: MUTATE the voice, MOVE the nav and the Reactor, BREAK the destruction, and a layer down BODY, AIR and DROWN (the room), each its own knob. 0 is the patch as programmed.
  • Twelve modulation slots: envelopes, two LFOs, RADAR, the nav, velocity, the wheel, aftertouch, random, and the engines themselves (the step, the bands, the Reactor's stage, the echoes).
  • 102 destinations: every knob worth patching, from PITCH to the envelopes' times, the filter, every engine (DISTROY's bands one by one: DRIVE, ALT, LEVEL, PAN, SPLIT), SUB, the looper (its speed an LFO's tape wobble, its level and sends), FREEZE as a gate and the frozen tail's level, SHIMMER, RADAR's SEARCH, the six macros, ARP's gate, and each slot's own AMOUNT: one slot opening another, a VCA on the cable. Picked from a list as DEST turns, each named by the page its knob is on and the knob's name there, in the pages' order.
  • An LFO's page: SHAPE, RATE, CLOCK, CYCLE; MOD sends it where it goes.

RADAR · a loop of random steps as a modulation source

  • A loop of 2–16 bits steps on the tempo, at its own DIV. SEARCH is the chance a bit flips as it comes round: locked at both ends, drifting between, the most in the middle.
  • Its newest 8 bits are a stepped value, OFFSET moving its middle. Where it goes is set in MOD: a slot with SOURCE RADAR, anywhere the matrix goes. To PITCH it is quantized to SEQ A's key first. DIV turned while it plays stays on the beat.
  • NOTES moves SEQ A's notes in the scale as they play; the pattern stays as you wrote it.
  • RADAR 1, a source of its own: the bits as on and off. From it, six more DESTs act on the 1s (or the 0s, K4 ON): RESTART the held notes' envelopes, NEXT the Reactor's stage, SEQ A, SEQ B and ARP play only there, REPEAT the looper's last slice, a stutter. AMOUNT is how often.
  • The picture: the loop as a scope, and its bits as a readout of 16 cells.

PLAYING IT · two tracks, ORBIT and an arpeggiator

  • A melody on A and a bass line on B: up to 64 steps each, with slides (each its own time, 30 ms to 1 s), ties, ratchets, chance and chords. SEQ A and B are MOD sources too: at 100 % to a pitch they play it in tune. REC records from the keys or a MIDI keyboard, at its velocity: step by step, or into the step that plays.
  • Parts: up to six A–F of a pattern (PARTS, a layer: K1 how many), each with its repeat, order, skip and length; SELECT picks one, the screen shows how they play.
  • ORBIT is the song: up to 16 steps of 1 to 8 loops, each naming the saved pattern A and B play there (at the one), transposing them, the loop and the frozen tail, and moving two lanes and the macros, jumping or gliding; at the end round again, or stop. 16 songs of your own.
  • MASTER (on SETUP): A or B, the track whose one leads. The other track and ORBIT switched on while it plays come in at its next one.
  • ARP from 1/64 to 1/2, triplets and dotted, on the patterns' grid while they play, and it stops with STOP (a held run comes back at START). REC on ARP keeps what it just played as a pattern.
  • The voices are shared by rules: no source cuts another's note.
  • MIDI clock in, over USB or the jack: the tracks, ORBIT, ARP, RADAR and the synced LFOs move by its pulses and never drift; START plays from step 1, STOP stops. SETUP: SYNC AUTO or INT. Without a clock in, the FM-1 sends its own over USB (START and STOP with PLAY), so a computer can follow it.

The pages

Six buttons, each with up to four pages, in the order the sound goes through them. Every page reads the same:

  • THE TOP LINE the patch and the key (from 90 % CPU a red warning in the patch number’s place, blinking from 100 %), then the button’s pages, the one shown red; right, EDIT’s lamp: ON or OFF
  • THE ROW K1–K4, left to right as the knobs: the layer ALGORITHM picked
  • THE PICTURE what the page does, drawn as it plays
  • THE HINT LINE ALGORITHM’s row: the page’s layers, the one in use lit; then the tempo, the octave and the voices

Twenty-one are new: each moves inside the note, through its four banks, and the wheel, aftertouch and velocity each have a job; none steps on the beat unless you ask MOVE or RADAR to. SAVE keeps 32 patches and 32 patterns of your own.

Install

  1. Back up your FM-1's flash first (M-Flasher or MvaveFM1Unbricker). Star Distroyer saves where the Felucca family keeps its data, and its first SAVE can overwrite another firmware's projects.
  2. Open this page in Chrome or Edge on a computer (they can talk to the FM-1 over USB MIDI).
  3. Connect the FM-1 directly to the computer with a USB data cable, and switch it on.
  4. Close other apps that use MIDI, tick the box, press Install and allow MIDI when the browser asks.
