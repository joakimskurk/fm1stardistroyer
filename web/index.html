▶ Play it in the browser · Hear the demos · Getting started

Star Distroyer turns the FM-1 into one instrument with four engines in a row. A voice of sixteen oscillators, morphed across an X/Y field; a Reactor of four resonators that sweep through vowels, chords and combs; DISTROY, which splits the sound into four bands and wrecks each its own way; and TIME and SPACE, a delay and a looper feeding a plate. Around them: a modulation matrix with a Turing machine, four macros, a two-track step sequencer with segments and ORBIT over it, and an arpeggiator.

Hear it

SINGULARITY, DERELICT and RANGE are played the way one person plays one FM-1: the app's own controls on their pages, the voice rules. SINGULARITY and RANGE are one pass each, nothing mixed in; DERELICT is four passes layered at fixed levels. CINEMATIC layers eight parts as a multitrack, each a stock patch on the engine. All four are rendered on a computer by the firmware's own sound engine, the same C the FM-1 runs. The picture under each is its spectrum, low to high, from start to end: tap it to jump.

Try it here

The firmware itself, compiled for the browser: the same C the FM-1 runs, its sound, its screen and its lights, on a panel laid out as the FM-1's. Turn it on and play: the keys with the mouse or a finger, the computer's keyboard or a MIDI keyboard; drag or scroll a knob to turn it. A click on SCL holds it until the next. What SAVE keeps stays in this browser.

POWER ON starts it: the firmware loads (a quarter of a megabyte) and plays here.

Getting started

The first minutes, on an FM-1 or in the player above. The player's computer keys are in brackets.

  1. Play. It starts on DISTROY, on the NAV page. Play the keys (A S D F … the white keys from C4, W E T Y U … the black ones, Z and X the octave), or a MIDI keyboard.
  2. Press PLAY (Space). SEQ A plays its starting line, a bass line in A minor: play over it. PLAY again stops it.
  3. Turn the macros. Press HOME until MACROS is the red tab on the top line. K1–K4 turn MUTATE, MOVE, BREAK and a fourth (BODY, AIR or DROWN, as the patch chooses). At 0 the patch is as it was programmed.
  4. Find a patch. Turn PRESETS: a list opens, and the sound stays as it is. EDIT loads the one picked, HOME keeps yours.
  5. Find your way. A page button goes to its page, and pressed again to its next one. K1–K4 turn the four values in the row on top. Hold SCL (Shift) for four more: their names turn red. EDIT turns what the page shows on or off, and ALGORITHM is the page's choice, named on the hint line.
  6. Write a pattern. On SEQ the white keys are the steps: tap one and turn K1 for its note, K4 for its type (a rest, a tie, a slide). Or press REC and play the keys: stopped, step by step; playing, into the step that plays. OCT moves the keys' octave here too; SCL + OCT turns the step bank.
  7. Keep it. Press SAVE, turn ALGORITHM to a place, and press SAVE again. On SEQ it keeps the pattern. In the player, what SAVE keeps stays in this browser.

The sound

VOICE · four banks of four oscillators

  • Each oscillator is a harmonic 1–32 of the note with a fine tune, a carrier or a modulator.
  • XM sets the FM, WARP turns sine → triangle → saw → pulse, FOLD folds, DETUNE spreads.
  • The NAV X/Y blends the four banks; COURSE plays four stored X/Y cells from each note.
  • Harmonic generators re-roll a bank. MONO, DUO or POLY.

REACTOR · a quad morphing resonator

  • Four filters of one model: BP6, BP12, a variable BPF, KP combs, or two kinds of allpass.
  • Six stages hold every filter's pitch and level; POS moves through them, SLEW jumps or glides.
  • A stage clock and key tracking; HARM snaps the filters to the note's harmonics.

DISTROY · multiband destruction

  • Four bands, LOW to TOP, each its TYPE (SOFT HARD FOLD CRUSH), DRIVE, what the type does (KNEE, SPACING, DEPTH), LEVEL, and BIAS, PAN and SPLIT on SCL.
  • PHAT lifts quiet parts and tails, SHARP spits transients.
  • A feedback loop with TAME to darken it, and a limiter so it never blows up.

TIME · SPACE · a delay and a looper into a plate

  • TIME, the delay: stereo or ping-pong (an echo left, then one as loud right), free (1 ms – 1 s) or on the beat, with feedback and tone. On the beat it stays there: a division longer than 1 s plays at half (TIME red).
  • LOOP (ALGORITHM on TIME): the looper, armed with L-LEVEL (it borrows half the delay line: 5 s, a light 8-bit tape, the delay then 0.5 s), always recording what you hear, plays its last LENGTH round while the delay goes on; SPEED takes it an octave up or down, as a tape's (SCL + OCT the next octave, over GLIDE, landing on the loop's own grid), REVERSE (SCL + ALGORITHM) plays it backwards; it goes into the reverb as the synth does.
  • SPACE, the plate: four diffusers into a tank in a figure of eight, its lines gently moving. SIZE 0.35–1.75 x (it glides there like tape), DECAY 0.3 s to about 40 s, TONE.
  • FREEZE (ALGORITHM on SPACE): the plate's tail held as it is, the input closed; play over it. SHIMMER (SCL + ALGORITHM): an octave up of the tail back into it.

MOVEMENT · macros and the matrix

  • Four macros: MUTATE the voice, MOVE the nav and the Reactor, BREAK the destruction, MACRO 4 as the patch chooses. 0 is the patch as programmed.
  • Eight modulation slots: envelopes, two LFOs, TURING, the nav, velocity, the wheel, aftertouch, random, and the engines themselves (the step, the bands, the Reactor's stage, the echoes).
  • 54 destinations, from PITCH to the envelopes' times, the filter, every engine (DISTROY's GAIN band by band and each of its three crossovers too, one going through another), the looper's speed (an LFO on it a tape's wobble; TURING in semitones) and the LFOs' rates, picked from a list as DEST turns.

TURING · a Turing machine as a modulation source

  • A loop of 2–16 bits steps on the tempo. CHANGE is the chance a bit flips as it comes round: 0 locks the loop, a little lets it drift.
  • Its newest 8 bits are a stepped value: DEST and AMOUNT send it anywhere the matrix can, XM by default, and in MOD it is a source for more. To PITCH it is quantized to SEQ A's key first.
  • NOTES moves SEQ A's notes in the scale as they play; the pattern stays as you wrote it.

PLAYING IT · two tracks, ORBIT and an arpeggiator

  • A melody on A and a bass line on B: up to 64 steps each, with slides, ties, ratchets, chance and chords. REC records from the keys or a MIDI keyboard, at its velocity: step by step, or into the step that plays.
  • Segments A–F cut a pattern on the white keys, each with its repeat and order.
  • ORBIT sequences the sequence: 2–8 steps of 1, 2 or 4 loops, each transposing A and B and moving two lanes and the macros, jumping or gliding.
  • MASTER (SCL + SEQ): A or B, the track whose one leads. The other track and ORBIT switched on while it plays come in at its next one.
  • ARP from 1/64 to 1/2, triplets and dotted, on the patterns' grid while they play. REC on ARP keeps what it just played as a pattern.
  • The voices are shared by rules: no source cuts another's note.
  • MIDI clock in, over USB or the jack: the tracks, ORBIT, ARP, TURING and the synced LFOs move by its pulses and never drift; START plays from step 1, STOP stops. SCL + PLAY: SYNC AUTO or INT.

Install

  1. Back up your FM-1's flash first (M-Flasher or MvaveFM1Unbricker). Star Distroyer saves where the Felucca family keeps its data, and its first SAVE can overwrite another firmware's projects.
  2. Open this page in Chrome or Edge on a computer (they can talk to the FM-1 over USB MIDI).
  3. Connect the FM-1 directly to the computer with a USB data cable, and switch it on.
  4. Close other apps that use MIDI, tick the box, press Install and allow MIDI when the browser asks.
